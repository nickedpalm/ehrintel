## How to Choose an EHR Consultant in Boise

- **Verify credentials before anything else.** CPHIMS, RHIA, and Epic Certified Implementation Consultant aren't vanity letters — they represent real exams and hands-on hours. In Idaho's smaller market, you'll encounter generalists who "know EHR" without ever sitting for a certification. Ask directly, then verify through AHIMA or HIMSS.
- **Match their experience to your EHR vendor.** A consultant who's implemented athenahealth a dozen times may struggle with a fresh Epic or Oracle Health rollout. Idaho's largest health systems (St. Luke's, Saint Alphonsus) have pulled Epic and Cerner talent into the region — find someone who's worked in that ecosystem, not just around it.
- **Ask about post-go-live support explicitly.** Most consultants are eager to close the implementation phase and move on. For smaller Boise practices — think the independent primary care groups along State Street or the specialty clinics in the East End — post-go-live optimization is where the real ROI lives. Get it in writing.
- **Check their MIPS and interoperability track record.** Idaho has a high proportion of independent rural-adjacent practices that need MIPS compliance baked in from day one. A consultant who hand-waves interoperability requirements is one who hasn't reckoned with the Idaho Health Data Exchange (IHDE) reality yet.
- **Get references from comparable practice sizes.** A consultant who's only worked enterprise-scale implementations may be completely lost in a 4-physician family medicine group. Boise's market skews toward mid-size independent practices — make sure your candidate has been there before.

> **Pro Tip:** Ask every candidate: "What's one thing you'd tell my practice to do *before* we start the implementation?" A consultant who gives you a real answer (assess your data quality, standardize your workflow documentation, identify your super-users early) is thinking about your success. One who pivots immediately to scope and timeline is thinking about theirs.

## What to Expect

EHR consulting engagements in Boise typically run $5,000–$50,000 depending on practice size, system complexity, and whether you need full-cycle support (vendor selection through post-go-live) or targeted help with a specific phase. Independent practices with 1–5 providers usually land in the $8,000–$20,000 range; larger multi-specialty groups adding data migration and staff training should budget toward the upper end.

> **Reality Check:** The most expensive mistake isn't hiring a consultant — it's hiring the wrong one and then paying a second one to clean it up. Lowball bids that skip the workflow assessment phase almost always generate scope creep that costs more than the "savings" you captured upfront.
