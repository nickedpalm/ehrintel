## How to Choose an EHR Consultant in Sioux Falls

- **Verify credentials before the first call.** CPHIMS, RHIA, and Epic Certified Implementation credentials aren't just alphabet soup — they signal someone who's passed a rigorous exam on health informatics standards, not just watched a vendor webinar. Ask for the credential number and verify it on AHIMA's registry.
- **Ask specifically about South Dakota Medicaid and MIPS experience.** SD Medicaid has its own quirks around claim formatting and eligibility verification that a consultant who's only worked in larger markets may not know. MIPS optimization is table stakes; SD-specific payer configuration is the differentiator.
- **Get a reference from a practice of similar size and specialty.** A consultant who crushed an Epic rollout for a 200-physician group at Sanford isn't automatically the right fit for a 6-provider family medicine clinic in Harrisburg. Scope match matters more than brand name.
- **Nail down the post-go-live support window in writing.** Most implementation disasters happen in weeks 3–8 after go-live, not during training. A 30-day included support period is the floor; 90 days is what you actually need.
- **Ask how they handle vendor negotiations.** The best consultants will tell you exactly which EHR vendors they've negotiated with and what contract traps to watch for — perpetual license fees buried in renewal clauses, data export restrictions, training hour minimums that don't reflect your workflow.

> **Pro Tip:** Sioux Falls has a growing cluster of direct primary care and concierge practices that often need lightweight EHR configurations rather than full enterprise builds. If your practice model is DPC or subscription-based, ask your consultant explicitly whether they've configured billing-lite or fee-forservice-off workflows — not every consultant has.

## What to Expect

EHR consulting engagements in Sioux Falls typically run $5,000–$50,000 depending on practice size, system complexity, and whether you're doing a fresh implementation, a platform switch, or targeted optimization work like billing workflow or interoperability fixes. A single-provider clinic migrating from paper to a cloud-based EHR might land at the lower end; a multi-specialty group switching from a legacy system with years of patient data to migrate will be closer to the top.

> **Reality Check:** The biggest pricing mistake practices make is scoping only the implementation and forgetting the data migration and training line items. Vendors quote software costs; consultants should be quoting total project costs. If a proposal doesn't include a data migration plan and a per-staff training hour estimate, you're looking at an incomplete scope — and you'll pay for the rest later, usually under pressure.
