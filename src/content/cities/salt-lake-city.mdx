## How to Choose an EHR Consultant in Salt Lake City

- **Verify credentials before anything else.** CPHIMS, RHIA, and Epic Certified Implementation Consultant aren't just alphabet soup — they're the difference between someone who's read the manual and someone who's done 40 go-lives. Ask for credential numbers and cross-reference with AHIMA or HIMSS directly.
- **Ask specifically about Utah Medicaid and UHIN interoperability.** Utah Health Information Network has its own connectivity requirements, and consultants who've worked in-state will already know the integration points. Out-of-state generalists often discover this on your dime.
- **Demand a vendor-agnostic assessment first.** Some consultants have referral relationships with specific EHR vendors. Nothing disqualifying about that — just know it going in. A solid consultant will assess your practice needs before they name a product. If they open with "have you considered Athena?" before they've seen your workflows, walk.
- **Check their MIPS and interoperability track record.** Utah has a high concentration of independent physician practices navigating Merit-based Incentive Payment System reporting. Consultants who've handled MIPS optimization in a live environment are worth a premium over those who've only done installs.
- **Get a scoped statement of work, not a handshake.** Engagements run $5,000 to $50,000+ — scope creep is how you end up at the high end without the results. A good consultant will hand you a phased SOW with clear deliverables before you sign anything.

> **Pro Tip:** Salt Lake City has a tight-knit healthcare administrator network. Before hiring, ask your consultant for two or three local references — not generic testimonials, actual practices in the SLC metro you can call. If they can't produce them, that tells you something.

## What to Expect

EHR consulting engagements in Salt Lake City typically run $5,000 for a focused optimization project (billing workflow, documentation cleanup) up to $50,000 or more for a full implementation — vendor selection, data migration, staff training, go-live support, and post-live stabilization. Most full implementations run 3–6 months from kickoff to go-live, though practices that arrive with clean data and clear internal champions move faster.

> **Reality Check:** The biggest pricing mistake practices make is scoping only for go-live and forgetting post-go-live support. The 90 days after you flip the switch are where productivity craters and billing errors compound. Any quote that ends at launch day is an incomplete quote — build in at least 30–60 days of stabilization support before you compare bids.
