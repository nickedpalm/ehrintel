## How to Choose an EHR Consultant in Manchester

- **Verify credentials, not just buzzwords.** CPHIMS and RHIA certifications from AHIMA and HIMSS are the baseline for serious consultants. Anyone who leads with "cloud-based solutions" and can't explain MIPS reporting requirements is a generalist wearing a specialist's hat.
- **Match the consultant to your EHR vendor.** A consultant with Epic Certified Implementation credentials is the right hire if you're joining a health network that mandates Epic. If your group is independently evaluating athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, or Kareo, you need someone with documented go-lives on *those* platforms — not a brand-agnostic generalist who will learn on your dime.
- **Ask specifically about New Hampshire's Medicaid interoperability requirements.** NH DHHS has specific HIE participation expectations tied to the New Hampshire Health Information Organization (NHHIO). A consultant unfamiliar with NHHIO integration has a blind spot that will surface at the worst possible time — post-go-live.
- **Check references from practices your size.** A consultant who specializes in 300-bed hospitals has a different mental model than someone who's optimized workflow for a 4-provider primary care group on Elm Street. Scope mismatch is the single most common reason implementations run over budget.
- **Get a fixed-fee proposal, not an hourly estimate.** Hourly engagements for EHR work balloon. The final invoice almost never looks like the initial estimate.

> **Pro Tip:** Manchester has a tight-knit medical community — the New Hampshire Medical Society and Greater Manchester Chamber both have provider networks. Ask a neighboring practice who they used and whether they'd hire them again. A warm referral beats cold vetting every time.

## What to Expect

EHR consulting engagements in Manchester typically run $5,000–$50,000 depending on practice size, vendor complexity, and how much of the implementation is turnkey versus consultant-led. A small independent practice doing a straightforward athenahealth migration might land at $8,000–$12,000; a multi-specialty group switching off a legacy platform with data migration and staff retraining can push $40,000 before the dust settles.

> **Reality Check:** The most common pricing mistake is hiring on day-rate without scoping the project deliverables. "We'll figure out the hours as we go" is how a $15,000 engagement becomes $35,000. Require a written statement of work with milestone-based payments before signing anything.
