## How to Choose an EHR Consultant in Charlotte

- **Verify credentials before the first call.** CPHIMS, RHIA, and Epic Certified Implementation Consultant aren't just alphabet soup — they're signals that someone has passed independent, rigorous testing. Ask to see the credential number and verify it on the AHIMA or HIMSS registry. Charlotte has plenty of "EHR advisors" who have neither.
- **Ask for vendor-neutral references — specifically.** Many consultants have preferred vendor relationships that pay referral fees. Ask directly: "Do you receive any compensation from EHR vendors?" Then ask for a reference from a practice that *didn't* go with that consultant's first recommendation.
- **Match specialty to practice type.** A consultant who's spent a decade on Epic implementations for large health systems may struggle with a 6-provider family medicine practice running eClinicalWorks. Charlotte has a mix of independent practices, FQHCs, and large hospital-affiliated groups — make sure your consultant's case history matches your setting.
- **Check for MIPS/interoperability experience.** North Carolina's Medicaid transformation (NC Medicaid Managed Care launched in 2021) created a complex web of interoperability requirements. Any consultant working in this market should understand NC HealthConnex, the state HIE, and how your EHR needs to connect to it.
- **Get a written scope of engagement before signing anything.** "Implementation support" can mean anything from weekly check-in calls to full-time on-site project management. Scope creep is the primary driver of EHR projects running over budget — pin down deliverables, milestones, and what's explicitly out of scope.

> **Pro Tip:** Ask every candidate: "What's the most common reason EHR implementations fail, and how do you prevent it?" The wrong answer is "bad software." The right answer involves staff adoption, workflow redesign, and change management. If they blame the vendor, keep looking.

## What to Expect

EHR consulting engagements in Charlotte typically run $5,000–$50,000 depending on practice size, scope, and whether you're doing a greenfield implementation, a platform switch, or optimization work on an existing system. A small independent practice doing a first-time EHR adoption is on the lower end; a multi-site specialty group migrating legacy data and rebuilding billing workflows is firmly in the upper tier. Most projects run 3–9 months from kickoff to post-go-live stabilization.

> **Reality Check:** The biggest pricing mistake practices make is scoping for go-live and forgetting about post-go-live optimization. The 90 days after launch — when staff are actually using the system — is where most efficiency gains (and losses) get locked in. If a consultant's proposal ends the day you flip the switch, budget separately for 60–90 days of follow-on support or you'll be back at square one.
