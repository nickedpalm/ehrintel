## How to Choose a EHR Consultant in Little Rock

- **Verify credentials before the first call.** CPHIMS and RHIA designations from AHIMA and HIMSS aren't honorary — they require documented experience and passing exams. A consultant without at least one active certification is almost certainly relying on vendor-side training alone, which means their recommendations will skew toward whoever pays them a referral fee.
- **Ask specifically about Arkansas Medicaid interoperability requirements.** Arkansas operates its own Health Information Exchange (Arkansas SHARE), and your consultant needs hands-on familiarity with it. If they pause when you mention SHARE, they're not the right fit for a state with this particular data-sharing infrastructure.
- **Distinguish between implementation consultants and optimization consultants.** If your practice is struggling with documentation inefficiency or MIPS reporting on an existing system, you don't need someone who specializes in go-lives — you need someone who's spent time post-go-live cleaning up what the implementation left behind. These are genuinely different skill sets.
- **Get references from practices your size in your specialty.** A consultant who's great at rolling out Epic for a 50-physician orthopedic group may be completely wrong for a 4-provider family medicine clinic switching from paper. Little Rock's medical community is tight-knit enough that a few calls to UAMS-affiliated practices or Baptist Health clinics can tell you a lot.
- **Nail down scope in writing before any contract is signed.** Vague statements like "full implementation support" have cost Arkansas practices tens of thousands in scope creep. Demand a line-item statement of work: vendor selection, data migration, staff training hours, and post-go-live support windows each get their own deliverable and timeline.

> **Pro Tip:** Little Rock has a notable concentration of Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs) and rural health clinics serving the Delta region. If your practice has any FQHC ties or sees a Medicaid-heavy patient panel, ask whether your consultant has specific experience with UDS reporting and HRSA compliance — most don't, and it matters.

## What to Expect

EHR consulting engagements in Little Rock typically run $5,000 for narrow optimization work (fixing a billing workflow, retraining staff on a specific module) up to $50,000 or more for full platform migrations involving data conversion, custom template builds, and six months of post-go-live support. Most mid-size practice engagements land in the $15,000–$30,000 range and span three to six months from discovery through stabilization.

> **Reality Check:** The most common pricing mistake is treating the EHR vendor's "free implementation support" as a substitute for an independent consultant. Vendor implementation reps are optimized to get you live on their timeline — not to redesign your intake workflow or make sure your MIPS denominator is pulling correctly. That gap is exactly where independent consultants earn their fee.

## Local Market Overview

Little Rock's healthcare market is anchored by UAMS, Baptist Health, and CHI St. Vincent — which means independent practices and specialty groups here often face negotiating EHR contracts with health systems that already have strong vendor preferences baked in. A consultant who understands that dynamic, and knows how to preserve clinical autonomy in integration agreements, is worth considerably more than one who treats every engagement as a clean-slate rollout.
