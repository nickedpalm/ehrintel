## How to Choose an EHR Consultant in Wichita

- **Verify credentials before the first call.** CPHIMS, RHIA, and Epic Certified Implementation Consultant aren't just alphabet soup — they signal that someone sat for a rigorous exam and works in this field professionally. Kansas has no state licensure for health informatics consultants, which means anyone can hang a shingle. Credentials are your filter.
- **Ask for Kansas-specific references.** A consultant who's implemented Epic at a 400-bed hospital in [Dallas](/locations/texas/dallas/) isn't automatically ready for a 6-provider independent practice in Wichita. Medicaid workflow requirements, Sunflower Health Plan integration, and KanCare billing nuances matter. Ask: *Have you worked with Kansas Medicaid before? What EHR did you implement, and can I talk to that practice?*
- **Match the consultant's EHR experience to your vendor shortlist.** If you're evaluating Athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, and NextGen, your consultant should have hands-on implementation hours with at least two of them — not a YouTube certification.
- **Scope before you sign.** The best consultants front-load a discovery phase: workflow mapping, staff interviews, billing audit. If someone jumps straight to recommending a vendor in the first meeting, they're selling, not consulting.
- **Check for conflicts of interest.** Some consultants take referral fees from EHR vendors. That's not automatically disqualifying, but it needs to be disclosed. Ask directly.

> **Pro Tip:** Wichita's healthcare landscape skews toward independent and small-group practices — family medicine, urgent care, behavioral health. A consultant with independent practice experience will move faster and cause fewer headaches than one whose resume is all health system mega-rollouts.

## What to Expect

EHR consulting engagements in Wichita typically run **$5,000–$50,000 depending on practice size, scope, and whether you need a full implementation or a targeted optimization**. A focused billing efficiency project for a 3-provider practice might clear at $6,000–$12,000. A full vendor selection plus go-live support for a 15-provider multispecialty group can push $35,000–$50,000 before you include any vendor-side implementation fees (which are separate). Timelines range from 6 weeks for a narrowly scoped engagement to 6–9 months for a complete platform migration with staff training.

> **Reality Check:** The most common budget mistake is scoping for implementation and forgetting post-go-live support. The first 90 days after an EHR launch are when workflows break, staff workarounds calcify, and billing drops 20–30%. Build post-go-live check-ins into your contract before you sign — not as a change order after things go sideways.
