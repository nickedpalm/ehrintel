## How to Choose an EHR Consultant in Seattle

- **Verify credentials, not just buzzwords.** Look for CPHIMS (the gold standard for health IT project management), RHIA (clinical data and compliance expertise), or Epic Certified Implementation Consultant status. Washington State has a strong HIPAA enforcement posture — you want someone who already knows the compliance landscape, not someone who learns it on your dime.
- **Ask about local vendor relationships.** Seattle is heavy Epic territory — UW Medicine, MultiCare, and Swedish all run Epic, which means a deep local talent pool with Epic fluency. If your practice is considering athenahealth, Modernizing Medicine, or a niche specialty EHR, ask directly whether their Epic experience translates or whether they're effectively starting from scratch.
- **Demand a workflow assessment before any vendor conversation.** Any consultant who opens with "I recommend [Vendor X]" before spending time in your practice is selling, not consulting. A real assessment covers billing workflows, documentation burden, referral patterns, and MIPS reporting obligations before a single demo gets scheduled.
- **Check references from similar-size practices.** A consultant who crushed a 50-provider hospital rollout may be completely wrong for a 4-physician family medicine practice. Seattle has both — make sure your reference calls are apples-to-apples on practice size and specialty.
- **Pin down the post-go-live scope upfront.** Implementation is the easy part to plan for. Where practices get burned is the 90-day optimization window after go-live: staff reverting to old workflows, billing errors stacking up, consultants who've already moved to the next engagement.

> **Pro Tip:** The Washington State Medical Association maintains HIT resource guides that can help you validate a consultant's local track record. Ten minutes of homework there has flagged more red flags than any LinkedIn profile ever will.

## What to Expect

EHR consulting engagements in Seattle typically run $5,000–$50,000 depending on scope — a vendor selection project for a small practice lands closer to the floor; a full migration with data conversion, interface builds, and staff training for a multi-specialty group hits the ceiling fast. Most engagements run 3–12 months from initial assessment to post-go-live sign-off, with the bulk of billable hours front-loaded in the discovery and implementation phases.

> **Reality Check:** The most common pricing mistake practices make is scoping the engagement only through go-live. Post-go-live optimization — fixing broken billing rules, retraining staff who reverted, tuning report templates — routinely adds 20–40% to the original budget. Get that scope defined in writing before you sign, or at minimum nail down a clear change-order process so you're not negotiating mid-project.

## Local Market Overview

Seattle's healthcare market is one of the most EHR-mature in the country — UW Medicine has been on Epic for decades, and that institutional history has created a genuinely deep local consultant pool. The flip side: independent practices in neighborhoods like Ballard, Capitol Hill, and Beacon Hill often run legacy systems that require consultants who can navigate both the technical migration and the cultural friction that comes with replacing software a practice has used for fifteen years.
