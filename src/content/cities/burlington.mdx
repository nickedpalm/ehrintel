## How to Choose an EHR Consultant in Burlington

- **Verify credentials before the first call.** CPHIMS, RHIA, and Epic Certified Implementation Consultant designations aren't honorary — they require documented experience and passing a proctored exam. Ask for the credential number and verify it directly with AHIMA or HIMSS. A consultant who hedges on this is a consultant you don't want.
- **Ask specifically about Vermont's RPC and VITL integration.** Vermont's Regional Primary Care Network and the Vermont Information Technology Leaders (VITL) health information exchange have specific onboarding requirements. A consultant who hasn't navigated VITL connectivity before is going to learn on your dime.
- **Demand a practice needs assessment before any vendor recommendation.** If a consultant leads with "I usually recommend Epic" before understanding your panel size, payer mix, and workflow, walk away. The needs assessment is the job — the vendor selection is a downstream output.
- **Check MIPS and quality reporting fluency.** Burlington's independent and multi-specialty practices are heavily exposed to MIPS penalties. Your consultant should be able to map your current documentation gaps to specific MIPS performance categories before the contract is signed.
- **Get references from practices your size.** A consultant with an impressive health system track record may be completely mismatched to a 6-provider independent practice. Vermont's healthcare market skews smaller — make sure their reference list reflects that.

> **Pro Tip:** Ask candidates how they've handled a go-live that went sideways. Every experienced consultant has a war story. The ones worth hiring will tell you exactly what broke, what they did, and what they'd do differently. The ones who've never had a hard go-live either haven't done enough of them or aren't being honest.

## What to Expect

EHR consulting engagements in Burlington typically run $5,000–$50,000 depending on scope — a focused workflow optimization or billing audit sits at the low end; a full-platform migration with staff training, data conversion, and post-go-live support runs significantly higher. Most engagements span 3–9 months from assessment through stabilization, though smaller practices adopting a first EHR can move faster with the right consultant driving the timeline.

> **Reality Check:** The most common pricing mistake is scoping for implementation and forgetting post-go-live support. The 90 days after launch are where revenue cycle problems surface, staff habits revert, and documentation quality falls apart. If your contract doesn't include a structured post-go-live optimization period, budget for one separately — or you'll be paying a consultant to fix preventable problems six months later at emergency rates.

## Local Market Overview

Burlington's healthcare market is compact but dense — UVM Health Network anchors the region and sets the interoperability standard that independent and specialty practices are measured against, which means smaller clinics face real pressure to implement systems that communicate cleanly with the academic medical center upstream. That dynamic makes vendor selection more consequential here than in markets where referral relationships are more fragmented, and it's exactly why getting the consultant selection right matters as much as the EHR selection itself.
