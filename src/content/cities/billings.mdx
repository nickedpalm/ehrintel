## How to Choose an EHR Consultant in Billings

- **Credential-match to your platform.** A consultant with an Epic Certified Implementation badge is not automatically qualified to handle your Athenahealth or NextGen migration. Ask for platform-specific go-live counts, not just years of experience. Three Epic go-lives beats twelve years of "various EHR work" every time.
- **Ask about MIPS and Montana Medicaid specifically.** Montana's Medicaid billing requirements have quirks that can break a workflow designed for a different state. A consultant who's worked inside RiverStone Health or SCL Health's regional systems will have this wired. One who hasn't will learn on your dime.
- **Verify CPHIMS or RHIA credentials directly.** AHIMA has a public credential verification tool. Use it. It takes 90 seconds and filters out a meaningful percentage of people who "studied for" the exam or let it lapse.
- **Get a timeline with milestones, not just a project end date.** Data migration, staff training, and post-go-live optimization are three distinct phases. If the SOW treats them as one blob, you'll have no leverage when things slip.
- **Ask who actually does the work.** Some firms sell you a senior consultant and staff you with a junior analyst. The name on the contract and the person in your clinic on day one should be the same person.

> **Pro Tip:** Billings Clinic and St. Vincent Healthcare have both run major EHR transitions in the last five years. A consultant with experience in either system — even as a subcontractor — has navigated Montana's specific referral networks, payer mix, and CAH (Critical Access Hospital) documentation requirements. That institutional knowledge is worth a premium.

## What to Expect

EHR consulting engagements in Billings typically run $5,000 for a focused optimization or billing audit up to $50,000+ for a full implementation with vendor selection, data migration, and go-live support. Expect a 90–180 day timeline for a mid-size practice switching platforms — shorter if you're optimizing an existing system, longer if you have messy legacy data or multiple locations.

> **Reality Check:** The cheapest bid is almost never the cheapest project. The consultants who underprice discovery and planning phases tend to find scope creep everywhere once work starts — and change orders are where thin margins become your problem. Get three quotes, compare scope line-by-line, and treat any proposal that skips a formal needs assessment as a yellow flag.
