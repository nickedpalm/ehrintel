## How to Choose an EHR Consultant in Wilmington

- **Check credentials first, portfolio second.** CPHIMS and RHIA are the gold standard for consultants working with clinical practices. CHDA matters if your pain point is analytics and reporting. Anyone pitching Epic implementation work without Epic Certified Implementation Consultant status is learning on your dime.
- **Ask specifically about Delaware Medicaid interoperability requirements.** Delaware's DHIN (Delaware Health Information Network) has specific data-sharing expectations — consultants who haven't worked in-state may underestimate the integration lift.
- **Get a post-go-live support commitment in writing.** Most implementation disasters happen in weeks 4–12, not during cutover. A good consultant prices in at least 90 days of post-launch support; anyone who disappears after go-live is a red flag.
- **Reference check with a practice of similar size and specialty.** A consultant who crushed it for a 40-provider hospital system may be completely wrong for a 3-physician family practice in Christiana. Scale matters.
- **Verify vendor neutrality.** Some consultants have referral arrangements with specific EHR vendors. Not inherently disqualifying, but you need to know before they steer your vendor selection.

> **Pro Tip:** Delaware has no state income tax on intangible assets, which means a lot of mid-Atlantic healthcare holding companies are incorporated here — which means Wilmington consultants often have exposure to multi-practice rollouts that pure regional players don't. Ask about it.

## What to Expect

EHR consulting engagements in Wilmington typically run $5,000–$50,000 depending on scope: a focused vendor selection project sits at the low end; a full implementation with data migration, workflow redesign, MIPS configuration, and staff training pushes toward the top. Most engagements span 3–9 months from kickoff to post-go-live stabilization.

> **Reality Check:** The single most common pricing mistake is scoping for the software purchase and underbudgeting for the human side — training, workflow redesign, and change management routinely cost as much as the implementation itself. If a quote seems suspiciously low, look at what it excludes, not what it includes.
